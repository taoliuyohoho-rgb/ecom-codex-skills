# Selling-Point Visual Contract

Use this contract when mapping approved product strategy into secondary ecommerce images.

## One Image, One Persuasion Job

- Keep slot 1 as the platform-compliant identity image.
- Give each secondary image one approved selling point or one buyer uncertainty. Do not split quantity, packaging, and product-detail views into several near-duplicate packshots when one image can answer the question.
- Bind each slot to `selling_point_id`, approved copy, fact source, visual mechanism, proof object, and exclusions. A missing fact source makes the claim slot `blocked`.
- Use one short headline and, only when needed, one support line. The image should remain understandable at marketplace thumbnail and mobile-detail sizes.

## Text Plus Visual

Use visible text when the visual alone cannot communicate the approved selling point precisely. The text states the claim; the visual makes it quickly understandable or emotionally legible. Neither layer may introduce a stronger claim than the approved source.

For secondary images, treat approved copy as a designed visual element rather than a fixed headline bar. Poster-like scale, cropping, hierarchy, material interaction, and unexpected placement are encouraged when the copy remains legible enough for the listing context. Preserve exact wording for commercial facts; freeform decorative copy must remain non-factual and cannot look like a claim, review, price, promotion, or certification.

Treat pack quantity, directions, test results, ratings, rankings, certifications, endorsements, prices, and promotions as commercial facts. They require a dated, reviewable source and must match the exact SKU, market, and variant.

## Review-Like Decision Support

Never fabricate a customer review, star rating, username, date, quotation, testimonial, or personal outcome. When real review evidence exists, aggregate it through the research evidence ledger and preserve the source boundary. When no review evidence exists, use a clearly labeled illustrative format such as `Common question`, `Before you buy`, or `What shoppers want to know`; write the concern and the verified answer without pretending that a customer said it.

Do not use a question-and-answer simply because its answer is defensible. The question must be a buyer uncertainty already evidenced by research or product truth, and the answer must make the decision clearer. Reject a defensive FAQ that introduces medicine, disease, safety, or treatment anxiety the buyer did not bring to the listing.

An unquoted, affirmative lifestyle thought is allowed as `non_factual_lifestyle_copy` when it supports a user-led desired-state or ritual slot. It must not look like customer speech or proof: no first-person result, stars, username, date, quote marks, testimonial card, rating, or outcome attribution.

## Pack Separation

Before generation, assign every slot a persuasion role: `identity`, `factual proof`, `physical inspection`, `procedure`, `need-state`, `desired-state`, `routine/ritual`, or `verified decision support`. See `pack-architecture.md` for the required differentiation matrix.

When two slots use the same product-plus-copy structure, the same life moment, or the same proof object, treat it as a pack-level failure unless their buyer questions and at least two other matrix fields are materially different. Change the mission before changing the art direction.

## Sensitive-Category Metaphors

For intimate care, sexual wellness, menstrual care, and adjacent personal-care products, use restrained non-anatomical metaphors when literal depiction would be intrusive or platform-inappropriate. Suitable mechanism families include hydration, softness, balance, protection, freshness, calm, and comfort expressed through water, fabric, light, abstract membranes, petals, or material texture.

Metaphors illustrate an approved idea; they do not prove efficacy. Do not use them to imply treatment, guaranteed outcomes, anatomical change, pathogen elimination, internal action, or clinical evidence. Avoid explicit anatomy, body fluids, sexualized posing, before/after body changes, fear imagery, and any metaphor likely to be read as a medical diagram. Do not copy a competitor's distinctive flower, artwork, person, or composition.

## Platform Adaptation

- Follow the target platform's current main-image rules; Amazon MAIN normally carries no added copy, badges, or metaphorical scene.
- Use text-plus-visual treatment primarily in secondary images or approved detail modules.
- When official directions are missing, mark How to Use as an evidence gap. Do not infer a sequence from a competitor or a metaphor.

## Review Questions

1. Does the image communicate exactly one approved selling point or buyer question?
2. Can every visible factual statement be traced to the exact SKU and market?
3. Does the visual support the text without overstating it?
4. Could a reasonable buyer mistake a metaphor for anatomical, clinical, or test evidence?
5. Is this slot materially different from the other eight images?
6. Does a decision-support question remove an evidenced uncertainty rather than create a new concern?
7. If the copy is a lifestyle thought, is it clearly non-factual and not framed as a customer review?
